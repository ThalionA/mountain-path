Nutrition is something that I've historically struggled with. My main issue is emotional eating, especially under stress, especially when it comes to sweet foods like chocolate, pastries, desserts, etc, but definitely also savoury things like pizza, crisps, etc. 

What I have found for myself, is that I feel pretty terrible the hours/days after bingeing on crap food, bloating, GI issues, worse sleep, and even [[brachialis-tendon-recovery|brachialis tendon flare-ups]]. This year has been much better so far, with fewer binge-restriction cycles. The biggest hit was during the spring trip to Font, with France having too much tempting food to resist over 2 weeks. During the [[alps2026|Alps trip of this year]], things were much better thankfully. 

In general, what I found works for me when I'm at home is surprise surprise... [[consistency]]. When I anchor my nutrition around a solid base of repeatable foods during the day, then I feel much better throughout the day (no big dips in energy), I have fewer cravings, I perform and recover great, and I'm happier overall with myself. These anchor foods are the following:
- **Breakfast**: Homemade protein bars (here's the [[homemade-protein-bar-recipe|recipe]]) - ~25g of protein for ~250kcal. I eat 1.5-2 each morning with my coffee. They taste great for me (now that I've nailed down the making process) and they can be packed super easily for trips too. The only problem is to not overeat them. 
- **Lunch**: Steak (200g) and rice (125g). I eat this almost every single day. It's perfect for me, because it fills me up nicely (which e.g. the meal deal from Sainsbury's doesn't do), is very quick to make (fry 4 steaks at a time, it takes 5min or so, I get ready microwaveable rice pouches), and has a good nutritional profile of protein and carbs. The steaks are usually Sirloin or Rump, bought at Lidl, because I'm not made of money.. ~50g of protein for ~435kcal!
- **Evening**: Skyr (200g) + Muesli (30g) for dessert after dinner. Not too heavy just before sleep, still pretty satisfying as dessert. The tricky bit is to not let the quantities drift upwards over time! ~20g protein, ~225kcal.
Apart from these staples of my current diet, there's almost always a few pieces of fruit, like apples, oranges, peaches, blueberries, raspberries, bananas, dried mango. The dinner usually revolves around meat or fish, with a carb source, and some fiber (I'm not the biggest lover of green stuff, but I try to have some "food that my food eats", like Ron Swanson would say).

In general, I aim for at least 2g of protein per kg of bodyweight, i.e. at least 140g or so but usually I sit even higher, like 160g. I supplement with 6g of creatine monohydrate (half in the morning coffee, half in the evening with a glass of water) daily. 

This very "boring" (according to my colleagues at the university) but consistent eating pattern works great for me and I can seemingly maintain it for months at a time.
