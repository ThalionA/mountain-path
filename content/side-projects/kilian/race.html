How well calibrated is this? (the model, the data, the residuals)

Every 50 m segment gets an energy cost per metre from its gradient: Minetti's treadmill curves uphill (scaled by a fitted uphill-economy factor), an empirical braking curve downhill, times a terrain factor. The runner brings a power budget that decays with total duration, P(T) = P1·(T/1 h)−k, reduced by altitude (−6.3 % per 1000 m above 300 m, steeper above 4000 m). Time is the sum of cost ÷ power, solved in closed form. P1, k and the uphill factor are fitted by least squares on log-time to the rows marked fit; the rows marked check are alpine and high-altitude records the fit never saw.

Reading the residuals: trail races from 2 h to 22 h land within about ±10 %; the fit trades his road speed (predicted a little fast) against Sierre-Zinal and Zegama (a little slow) — Kilian is a better mountain runner than his flat times imply and no single power curve captures both. Above ~5500 m (Denali, Aconcagua) the model over-promises by 20–30 %: unacclimatised extreme altitude, cold and snow are not modelled. Course distances for the older FKTs are approximate where marked.

Sources: fastestknowntime.com athlete page; race results from iRunFar, Trail Runner, World Athletics and race organisers; Minetti et al. 2002 J Appl Physiol 93:1039; Wehrlin & Hallén 2006 Eur J Appl Physiol 96:404.
