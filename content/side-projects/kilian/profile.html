How accurate is this?

The sample batch is a runner simulated with known qualities, so the page can be marked against an answer sheet. Given a correct max heart rate it recovers the engine to within about 3 %, and uphill economy to within about 6 %. Two honest failures: if the max heart rate is guessed from training runs it reads roughly 10 % low and drags the engine about 17 % down with it; and the descent model has a single degree of freedom, which sets how steep and gentle descents differ but cannot express a runner who is simply slower on all descents — for that runner the leftover error lands in the engine, about 9 % in the test. Endurance decay only comes from your own efforts if you have gone hard at several durations; otherwise the page says it assumed a value. The measured halves of this page — pace by gradient, climb rates, heart-rate mix, personal bests, trends — carry none of that modelling risk.

Method, in short

Each run is cut into 50 m segments with gradient, altitude, your time, heart rate (lagged 60 m) and cadence. The energy cost of a segment comes from the same model as Race Kilian; implied power is cost ÷ time and "flat-equivalent pace" is the flat pace that would cost the same. Engine: a per-run fit of flat-equivalent speed against heart rate, read at the reference HR. Climbing: median vertical speed per gradient band; walking is cadence below 124 steps/min. Descending: median speed per band against your flat speed; braking is the spread of speed within descent legs. Durability: per-beat power in the second half of a run versus the first (first 10 min dropped). Your uphill economy and descent factor are the cost-curve parameters that make your per-beat power constant across gradients; your endurance decay comes from your best efforts by duration; your engine from HR-scaled runs (hard runs weighted most) or from a race result if you give one. Hikes are excluded by activity type or implied power below 6.5 W/kg. The club runner is synthetic.
