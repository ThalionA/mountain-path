## The route

South from the Cabane de Moiry across the **Glacier de Moiry** to the plateau at ~3000 m, then west to gain the **NE ridge** at around pt. 3274, marked by a rock gendarme, and along it to the east summit.

The crux is the **first ~100 m of the ridge** — weathered rock, few solid holds, the sort of ground where a helmet stops being optional. Above that the granite gendarmes are either traversed or bypassed and the climbing improves.

## What actually happened

Away at 05:00 across the glacier. I led the ridge pitch, which went fine; building the anchor at the top of it took me far too long, and that slowness is the honest finding of the day.

We summited in 4 h 30, then **extended along the ridge toward the Dent des Rosses** — a long three-dimensional scramble with two rappels to get off it. The first was built off the summit block onto the ridge; the second, down to the glacier, went off a fixed anchor found by a smear of red paint, and the **rope came up just short**, so we downclimbed the last section.

The consequence: we were back on the crevassed glacier at about **14:00, in soft isothermal snow**, with a freezing level of 4500–4800 m and weakening snow bridges flagged in the forecast a week earlier. The rule I had written before the trip was *off the summit by 13:00*, and it existed for exactly this. We glissaded out, reached the hut at 15:40, and got down to the Moiry dam in an hour ahead of an incoming storm.

Good day. Wrong margin.
