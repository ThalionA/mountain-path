The first Alpine summit I ever stood on, and deliberately the easiest thing on the [[alps2026|Alps 2026]] list - essentially an acclimatisation day and testing that our gear etc work.

## The route

Out of the Cabane de Moiry heading south-east then east onto the glacier, up to the **Col du Pigne (3140 m)**, then south along the **NW ridge** to the summit. Descent by SSW ridge. Just before reaching the glacier, there was a section of wet rock slabs that felt sketchy for me to descend using crampons. Still have to get used to trusting crampons on rock. 

No real difficulties on the ridge scramble, some loose rocks, but no real exposure etc. Helmet on from the col.

## What actually happened

Away from the hut at 05:40. Warm enough to climb in a t-shirt. A bit too warm in the entire trip. I essentially never used a down jacket, and only wore my gloves a little on glaciers. We stayed roped but barely placed anything since the ridge is a scramble, not a pitch.

The lesson of the day was the **descent**, not the ascent. Coming down the wet rocky slabs in crampons felt considerably sketchier than "F" suggests. In general, descending always felt harder than ascending, and mixed rock/snow/ice terrain always felt sketchier than any of those in isolation to me.
