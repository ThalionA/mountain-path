## The route

South from the Cabane de Moiry across the **Glacier de Moiry** to the plateau at ~3000 m, then west to gain the **NE ridge** at around pt. 3274, marked by a rock gendarme, and along it to the east summit.

A relative crux was just gaining the ridge. We had two teams ahead of us on the day, first a team of 4 French climbers, and then a team of 4 British climbers. Arriving at the base of the ridge, we had to wait until both parties made it to the ridge to start climbing ourselves, since there was a quite a bit of loose ground. We easily lost at least 30min waiting. A short, loose pitch led to the ridge. Going up the ridge is really nice, fun, easy scrambling up. 

## What actually happened

Away at 05:00 across the glacier. I led the ridge pitch, which went fine; building the anchor at the top of it took me far too long, and that slowness is the honest finding of the day.

We summited in 4h30min, then **extended along the ridge toward the Dent des Rosses**, a very engaging, if a bit long, three-dimensional scramble with two rappels to get off it. The first was off an anchor at the summit onto the ridge; the second, down to the glacier, was from a fixed anchor found by a smear of red paint, and the **rope came up just short**, so we downclimbed the last section. We potentially missed another anchor halfway down to the glacier, since some sources say it's a 2x 25m rappel?

The consequence: we were back on the crevassed glacier at about **14:00, in very soft snow**, with a freezing level of 4500–4800 m and weakening snow bridges flagged in the forecast a week earlier. This was definitely not ideal, and the increasing lateness was on my mind during the entire scramble. We glissaded out, reached the hut at 15:40, and got down to the Moiry dam in an hour ahead of an incoming storm.

The best day of the trip, but we might have been lucky to not get into more trouble with crevasses etc.
