We live in a time where everything has to be fast, including your progress in climbing, and we're bombarded with content telling us how things can be done in record times. This is the opposite argument: a 25–40 minute climbing-specific warmup that doubles as my supplemental strength training. Hammer curls, weighted pullups, max hangs on the fingerboard, interleaved with mobility work during the 5-minute rests. Two consequences of slowing down: my finger and elbow tendons stay healthy ([[brachialis-tendon-recovery|important context]]), and I bring a calmer, more focused state to the actual climbing, which matters more for hard sessions than I used to think.

Here is how my warmup is structured (the numbers below are specific to me, scale appropriately):
	- Potentially jump rope - 60-90sec (now that I cycle most times, I don't need to do this step)
	- Reverse curls (same as for [[brachialis-rehab-schedule|brachialis rehab]]) with 5kg
	- Pullups - 2 sets x 5reps at BW, 5x +15kg, 5x +30kg, 3x +45kg or 2x +50kg
	- Hangs - 2 sets x 7sec at bw, 20mm, half crimp and 3 finger drag, then with same weights as for pullups
	- In between these sets (5min or so), stretches:
		- Hip flexor stretch
		- Hamstring stretch
		- Pigeon pose
		- Cossack squats
		- Box stretch
		- Frog
		- Pancake
		- Butterfly
		- Spine twists
	- Potentially a few campus moves just before end of warmup
	- Potentially a few campus jumps on smaller edges just before end of warmup
