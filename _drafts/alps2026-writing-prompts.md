# Alps 2026 — prompts

Not a draft. Raw material only. Write **under** each prompt, in the blank space.

**Three rules while writing:**
1. Timer on. When it rings, stop mid-sentence.
2. No editing, no re-reading, no deleting.
3. Ugly is fine. Nothing here goes on the site as-is.

**You are not starting from zero.** You already wrote ~6,000 words on this trip:
- `ExpeditionVault/Expeditions/2026-06_Alps_Walliser-MonteRosa/Journal/` — 7 entries, 22–30 June
- The hole on the site that's already shaped for this: `content/expeditions/alps2026.md`

*(Decide once, before you publish: how you name your rope partner publicly. Then stop thinking about it.)*

## Tier 0 — 3 minutes each. Sentences are BANNED.

Fragments, lists, single words. If you write a full sentence you've broken the rule.

### 0.1 — Twenty nouns
Twenty concrete things from the trip. Objects, places, sounds, foods. No adjectives, no explaining.

<br><br><br>

### 0.2 — The table
One row per peak: name · date · time hut-to-hut · grade · **one word**.

### 0.3 — Steal from yourself
Open the journal. Find the **three sentences you already wrote** that deserve to be on the site. Paste them here. That's the whole task — copying counts as writing.

<br><br><br>

### 0.4 — Topo the trip
Describe the whole ten days as if it were a single route in a guidebook: name, grade, length, conditions, "beware of".

<br><br>

### 0.5 — Three sentences to a friend
The trip, to someone who wasn't there, in exactly three sentences. Say it out loud first, then type what you said.

<br><br>

## Tier 1 — 15 minutes each. One paragraph. 150–250 words.

Pick whichever one you can already hear yourself saying. Skip the rest.

### 1.1 — The sound (concrete)
Crampons on rock. Day one it was "screechy" and unpleasant. By the Vincent ridge you could hear *solid / ok / marginal* in it. Write only about that: what changed, over how many hours, and how you knew. Don't explain why it matters.

### 1.2 — Perceptual learning, from the inside (abstract)
Same event, other hat: you spend your working life on how brains build discriminations from noise, and you just ran the experiment on yourself, n=1, six days, no controls. Write the paragraph you'd write if this were data. Where would you refuse to believe it?

### 1.3 — "More sketched out by the people than the mountain"
Bishorn. Write down **only what you saw** — rope behind legs, prusiks that wouldn't hold, half-buried in a crevasse for the photo. No verdict, no adjectives about the people. Let the list do it.

### 1.4 — The day you didn't write about
There's no journal entry for 24 June, Pointes de Mourti, 10h40, "epic". You were too tired. Write it now, two months late. Start with the moment you remember most sharply and go outwards — not chronologically.

### 1.5 — The crux, in slow motion
The slab, the step left around the block, the two bits of cord in drilled holes. 200 words on those ninety seconds. Present tense.

### 1.6 — Why climb (you've already argued this)
On 29 June you wrote the argument out in one go. **Don't rewrite it — cut it.** Delete every judgement of another person and every "I guess". Get it to 150 words. What survives is the piece.

### 1.7 — Overstepping
"I wonder a bit if we overstepped our abilities today." You asked it and then answered it three ways in the same paragraph. Write the version where you commit to one answer. If you can't, write why you can't — that's more honest and more interesting.

### 1.8 — The summit that didn't happen
Signalkuppe. Timeline only. Bullets. Times, weather, what each of you said, who said it first. No feelings, no justification. (The feelings can come in a second pass, or never.)

### 1.9 — What you got wrong
Your own expeditions index promises failures get equal billing. Three things you did badly that a competent alpinist wouldn't have. Start with the crampons.

### 1.10 — Absurdity (abstract)
"Standing on top of a mountain is almost entirely useless in itself." Take that seriously for 250 words instead of resolving it. Don't rescue the ending.

## Tier 2 — the actual post. 60–90 minutes, or three Tier-1 blocks glued together.

### 2.A — Route report *(lowest energy — structure does the work)*
One section per peak. Fixed headings, filled in like a form: **Objective · Conditions · What happened · What I'd change.** Deliberately unliterary. Publishable in one sitting.

### 2.B — Seven days, seven paragraphs
One paragraph per journal entry, 100 words each, in order. Hard cap. The constraint is the whole trick.

### 2.C — One thread through ten days
Pick a single thread — the sound of metal on rock, the crowds, the fear, the question of why — and follow only it. Everything that doesn't serve it gets cut. This is the essay version.

### 2.D — The letter
Write it to one specific person who wasn't there and knows nothing about alpinism. Then delete the greeting and the sign-off. What's left is the post.

## If you have 45 minutes and want a post at the end of it

1. **0:00–0:05** — Tier 0.2 (the table) and 0.3 (steal three sentences).
2. **0:05–0:20** — one Tier 1 prompt. Any one.
3. **0:20–0:35** — a second Tier 1 prompt.
4. **0:35–0:45** — paste all of it into `content/expeditions/alps2026.md` under a `## Trip report` heading. Unedited. Publish or don't.

Editing is a different job for a different day. Don't do both.
